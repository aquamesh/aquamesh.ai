AquaSpectra™ smart probe

What conductivity cannot tell you.

Conductivity says there are ions in the water. It cannot say whether they are harmless salts or the product you just carried out of a rinse tank. The spectrum separates the two.

  • Organic carryover, separated from salts The spectral response distinguishes organic load from dissolved solids, which a single conductivity reading cannot do.
  • Early indication on hydrocarbons Aromatic and hydrocarbon content give a distinct optical response — useful as an early warning ahead of a discharge sample.
  • Continuous through dirty water Built to keep reading in fouling-prone streams rather than needing a clean, conditioned sample.
  • Reagent-free and in place No sample line to maintain and nothing to refill, so it can sit on a stream nobody wants to service weekly.

What we find

What usually turns up on the water side.

These four turn up in most plants we look at. The examples beneath each are the shapes they take in manufacturing — your own data decides which are real at your site.

  • Product and utilities leaving in the water

    Water is usually the last stream anyone watches, which is why recoverable value tends to leave through it.

    • Product or additive carried into a rinse or wash stream
    • Reusable water going to drain because nothing measures its quality

  • Parallel equipment drifting apart

    Two circuits on the same duty should look the same. Divergence is usually the first sign of a problem.

    • Parallel cooling circuits behaving differently under the same load
    • Two lines producing different effluent from the same recipe

  • Settings that cover a worst case every day

    Where the risk is invisible, the safe setting is chosen — and then paid for continuously.

    • Blowdown and chemical dosing set to cover the worst case
    • Chiller or compressor setpoints inherited from the last engineer

  • Instruments that stopped telling the truth

    Between scheduled samples, an instrument nobody checks is an assumption.

    • A conductivity probe reading flat for months
    • Duplicate meters that no longer agree with each other
