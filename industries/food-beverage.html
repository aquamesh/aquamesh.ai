5–15%+ target savings, across every line of the P&L.

  • Energy

    Refrigeration, compressed air, pumps and heat recovery run to what the process needs, not to last summer's setpoint.

The short answer

The fastest way to find product and water losses going down the drain.

Start with the historian data you already have. Line up effluent load against cleaning and changeover records, and the losses show up as load that continues after the line has finished its work. No new hardware is needed for that first look.

  1. 1

    Export what you already log

    Pull effluent flow, organic load, solids and turbidity from the historian, with the CIP and production records for the same period.

  2. 2

    Line up load against events

    Place changes in effluent load beside changeover and cleaning timestamps, so each rise is tied to what the line was doing at the time.

  3. 3

    Look for the two common shapes

    A cleaning cycle that keeps running after the return line has come clean, and a changeover flush cut wider than the transition needs.

  4. 4

    Rank, then measure where you are blind

    Rank the findings by what each is worth a year. Where existing instruments cannot see a stream, one or two probes confirm it. Your operators decide what to change.

How much is typically lost

Published dairy research puts routine losses at 1–3% of the milk a plant processes, mostly at startups, changeovers and rinses. What your plant loses depends on the product, the line and the cleaning regime. The free audit on your historian export gives you that figure, with the tags that are the evidence.
